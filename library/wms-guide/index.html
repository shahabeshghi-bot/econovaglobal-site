WMS readiness guide

A warehouse management system amplifies whatever it finds. This guide is what to settle before selection, so the system lands on sound processes, clean data and requirements you chose, not a vendor’s template.

1. Fix the process before you automate it

Map receiving, put-away, slotting, picking, packing and dispatch as they actually run. Where the current process is broken (workarounds, double handling, knowledge held by one person), correct the design first. Configuring a WMS around a broken process produces a faster broken process, with licence fees.

2. Get the data honest

A WMS runs on master data: SKU dimensions and weights, pack sizes, location addresses, barcode discipline. Audit the data before migration, and measure your inventory accuracy by cycle count. Go-live on inaccurate stock records is the most common self-inflicted wound in WMS projects.

3. Write requirements from your order profile

Your channels decide the requirements: full-pallet B2B, foodservice case picking, e-commerce single-unit fulfilment, VAS like kitting and labelling, cold chain checks, DSD dispatch. List the processes the system must support, in the volumes and shapes you actually ship, then weight vendor evaluations against that list.

4. Select independently

Every vendor demo is choreographed around the vendor’s strengths. Insist on scripted scenarios drawn from your own operation, your ugliest order, your peak day, your returns flow, and score every candidate against the same script. Selection done by an adviser with no software to sell is selection you can defend to your board.

5. Plan go-live as an operation, not an event

Training, UAT with real orders, a cut-over plan, and hypercare staffing for the weeks after. Then, once the system has bedded in, run a post-implementation audit: did the project deliver what the business case promised? It is the step almost everyone skips and the only one that proves the investment.

The mistakes that cost the most

  • Buying features for the operation you wish you had, instead of the volumes you ship.
  • Automating undocumented processes and discovering the exceptions at go-live.
  • Migrating inventory records that were never verified.
  • No baseline: without pre-project KPIs, “improvement” becomes an opinion.
  • Ending the project at go-live, no monitoring, so the gains drift away within a year.
